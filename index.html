01

How it works

Every person needs a different optimization: the layers, their initialization and their order depend on what the person wears. AvatarAnyone lets AI agents read the structure of the person and build the optimization, and a joint optimization fits all layers.

The method in about a minute. Click a chapter to jump to it.

03

Cloth simulation

Each avatar's own dress or skirt is a separate garment layer, so it can be simulated as cloth over the animated body while the avatar dances: here with Blender's cloth simulator, rendered in Blender with Cycles in scenes lit by their environment maps. Five avatars, five dances.

04

Avatars in Unreal Engine

The avatars import into Unreal Engine as skinned meshes and play motion-capture clips in game scenes. Collision bodies that follow the skeleton let Unreal's physics react to them: a thrown ball, a kicked ball, falling beach balls, crates and traffic cones.

05

Physics in MuJoCo

In the robotics simulator MuJoCo, convex collision hulls built from the avatar's own mesh follow a motion-capture clip and push simulated objects: walking through a wall of boxes, capoeira among box towers, jumping jacks under falling objects, punching a spring-mounted pad, wiping cans off a table.

Comparison with the state of the art

Reconstructions from YouTube videos with occlusions, used in our perceptual study. All six methods start from the same canonical image; we compare with three single-image human methods (PSHuman, SyncHuman, HumanNOVA) and two general image-to-3D models (TRELLIS.2, Pixal3D). Every result is rigidly aligned to ours and all six are rendered through one camera; the inset zooms on the face.
